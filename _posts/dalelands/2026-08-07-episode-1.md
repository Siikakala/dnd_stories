Welcome back to the campaign companion blog! After setting up our table culture, house rules, and Level 1 character concepts in Episode 0, it is time to kick off the official narrative arc of our multi-year Dalelands campaign.

If you are coming from D&amp;D 5th Edition, low-level play in older editions like D&amp;D 3.0e feels distinctly gritty. There are no bonus-action *Healing Words*, no death save safety cushions, and zero room for reckless errors. Here is how our story officially began on the 1st of Marpenoth, DR 1372.

---

## 1\. The Campaign Begins (1–3 Marpenoth, DR 1372)

Our adventure opens in the Dalelands during the autumn of DR 1372\. On the 1st of Marpenoth, the party gathers in the local region as political tension and minor wilderness threats hover over the countryside.

By the 3rd of Marpenoth, Jackson reunites with his brother and officially connects with the rest of the adventuring group. Like many classic D&amp;D campaigns, the heroes don't start by fighting demigods or dragon-knights—they start with a grounded local problem. A local named Belran has lost his horse, and the party agrees to investigate what happened.

---

## 2\. D&amp;D 3.0e Mechanics Spotlight: Wilderness Tracking vs. 5e

For 5e players, tracking a lost horse or runaway bandits is as simple as asking your Ranger or Druid to roll a Wisdom (Survival) check. In D&amp;D 3.0e, however, wilderness navigation was far more specialized:

* **The Track Feat Requirement**: In 3.0e, you could not follow tracks with a DC higher than 10 unless your character specifically possessed the **Track** feat. Without it, even a high-Wisdom character could only spot the most obvious footprints.
* **Wilderness Lore Skill**: Tracking checks relied on the *Wilderness Lore* skill (which was later renamed *Survival* in 3.5e). Modifiers were applied for surface conditions (soft mud vs. hard rock), track age, group size, and light levels.
* **Lethality at Level 1**: At Level 1, a single longbow arrow or shortsword attack roll could put a character at negative hit points. The party had to approach even routine tracking missions with genuine caution.

---

## 3\. The Bandit Ambush &amp; Interrogating the Prisoners

Following the trail into the brush, the party encounters a group of bandits responsible for taking Belran's horse. Utilizing tactical positioning and quick physical engagement, the party overcomes the bandits and takes several survivors captive for questioning.

Upon interrogation, the captured bandits yield crucial information:

* Their leader, **Boss Draff**, is no longer at the camp.
* Draff departed earlier alongside an associate named **Galath**.
* Fresh tracks outside the hideout confirm that Draff and Galath have headed further into the surrounding wilderness.

---

## 4\. Character Growth &amp; Next Steps

This first encounter establishes the initial bond between the party members. Jackson's protective instincts toward his brother, paired with the group's emerging tactical synergy, set the groundwork for how this party operates under pressure.

With Boss Draff and Galath on the loose, the party prepares to follow the fresh trail deeper into the Dalelands. Join us in Episode 2 as the investigation expands into larger regional threats and ancient dwarven secrets!
