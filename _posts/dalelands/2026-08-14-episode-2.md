## 1\. Deciphering the Dwarven Statuette: The Legacy of Fyrfar Smokebeard

As documented in the session notes from 25 November 2019, the scholar Arvien completed copying and translating the dense runes etched into the cryptic dwarven statuette recovered from Haresk and Holfast’s mysterious wooden box.

The statuette depicts **Fyrfar Smokebeard**, an ancient master dwarven smith credited with forging **Sargathuld**—a legendary blade crafted specifically for slaying orcs and dark forces. Arvien's notes confirm that Sargathuld was once wielded by the legendary adventurer band known as *Grimblade*, before being lost during an ill-fated raid into the dragon-infested depths of Cormanthor.

### Mechanics Corner: 3.0e Decipher Script vs. 5e Arcana/History Checks

For players who started their tabletop journey in 5th Edition, translating ancient texts usually involves a simple Intelligence (History) or Intelligence (Arcana) check, or casting *Comprehend Languages*. In D&amp;D 3.0e, however, written translation was its own distinct skill: **Decipher Script**.

* **Decipher Script (3.0e)**: Characters had to allocate specific skill ranks to Decipher Script (an Intelligence-based skill). Translating an archaic or exotic dialect—such as ancient High Dwarven runes—required a DC 20 to DC 30 check. A failed roll by 5 or more meant the reader misread the passage, potentially leading to false clues!
* **Knowledge Skills in 3.0e**: Unlike 5e's broad five knowledge skills (Arcana, History, Nature, Religion, Investigation), 3.0e had ten distinct Knowledge sub-skills, including *Knowledge (Architecture and Engineering)*, *Knowledge (Dungeoneering)*, and *Knowledge (Local)*. Arvien’s work required cross-referencing *Knowledge (History)* with *Decipher Script* to pinpoint Fyrfar Smokebeard’s historical era.

## 2\. The Bargain at Holfast's Inn: Noristuor's Request

According to campaign entries from 7 January 2020, the party reconvened at **Holfast's Inn** in Ashabenford to figure out how to reach the long-abandoned **Mines of Tethyamar**—a vast subterranean complex rumored to contain priceless dwarven relics, mithril veins, and ancient magical weapons.

Navigating the wilderness toward Tethyamar is no simple task for a low-level party. At the inn, the adventurers made contact with **Noristuor**, an enigmatic local broker and wilderness scout who possesses crucial intelligence on safe passages toward Tethyamar.

Noristuor expressed willingness to guide and assist the party, but he isn't interested in simple gold coin. Instead, he demanded payment in raw magical components: specifically, the **eye of a magical beast**.

### Mechanics Deep-Dive: What is a Ravid in 3.0e?

5e players are likely familiar with Sea Lions (or Giant Sea Captains/Beasts) and Orc Shamans, but the **Ravid** is a classic 3.0e creature that rarely appears in modern manuals.

* **Plane of Positive Energy**: In 3.0e lore, Ravids originate from the Positive Energy Plane. They resemble long, shimmering, serpent-like creatures with a single arm-like appendage near their head.
* **Feat &amp; Ability — Animated Objects**: Ravids passively emanate positive energy that spontaneously brings nearby inanimate objects to life. Walking into a ruined wizard's tower guarded by a Ravid means fighting animated chairs, floating spellbooks, and stone gargoyles simply by proximity!
* **Positive Energy Lash**: While positive energy heals living creatures in small doses, a Ravid’s natural touch attack channels raw life-force so intensely that it causes positive energy overload, dealing heavy damage to mortals and destroying undead instantly.

## 4\. Gearing Up &amp; Into the Wilds: The First Leg of Travel

As noted in the campaign records from 13 January 2020, the party spent a day buying extra provisions, sharpening weapons, and securing iron rations in Ashabenford before setting out.

The notes record that the first two days of wilderness travel passed quietly, with no monster ambushes or Zhentarim patrols interrupting their march. This calm before the storm gave the party time to coordinate their combat strategies, discuss which of Noristuor's three beast targets to hit first, and digest the revelation of Fyrfar Smokebeard’s statuette.

### 3.0e Wilderness Travel Rules

In 5th Edition, travel is usually handled by choosing a Fast, Normal, or Slow pace with passive Perception checks. In 3.0e:

* **Encumbrance &amp; Armor Check Penalty**: Carrying heavy gear or wearing medium/heavy armor directly reduced a character's base tactical movement speed (e.g., from 30 ft down to 20 ft).
* **Forced Marching**: Travelling more than 8 hours a day required hourly Constitution checks (DC 10 + 1 per extra hour). Failure dealt subdual (non-lethal) damage and left characters fatigued, suffering a -2 penalty to Strength and Dexterity.
* **Survival / Wilderness Lore**: Checking for forage and water while moving required daily rolls against DC 10 to DC 15 depending on terrain difficulty.
